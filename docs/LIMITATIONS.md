# Limitations

What this platform cannot tell you, and why. Read this before quoting any number
from it.

Everything here is measured, not assumed. Counts come from
`data/derived/quality_report.parquet` (generated by `scripts/10_quality.py`) and
`data/derived/tracking_quality.parquet`.

---

## 1. Sample size is the dominant limitation

**Ten games.** Full tracking and events exist for 10 of the 327 ACB 2025/26
games. Every tracking-derived number in this project rests on that.

| Unit | Total | Per team | Per player |
|---|---|---|---|
| Games | 10 | 1–2 | 1–2 |
| Chances | 2,055 | 94–210 | — |
| Shots | 1,459 | ~86 | 10–30 |
| Picks | 1,520 | ~89 | 15–75 |
| Drives | 660 | ~39 | 8–27 |
| Closeouts | 601 | ~35 | 8–25 |
| Tracked minutes | — | — | 8–40 per player |

Practical consequences:

* A player's FG% on 15 attempts has a standard error of about **13 percentage
  points**. Two players separated by less than that are indistinguishable.
* Points per drive on 10 drives has a standard error of about **±0.35**. The
  drive leaderboard is a description of ten games, not a skill ranking.
* Team-level comparisons cover **one or two games per team**. The scouting page
  is a scouting *format* demonstrated on a small sample, not a season report.
* No causal claim in this project is defensible. Where a relationship is
  reported, the direction, the confidence interval and the sample size are
  printed alongside it.

The app enforces minimum-sample gates (8 drives, 15 picks, 8 closeouts, 10
attempts, 8 tracked minutes) and prints sample sizes next to every leaderboard.

## 2. Broadcast tracking is not motion capture

Positions are extracted from broadcast video by computer vision. Measured across
the ten games:

| | value |
|---|---|
| Positions **directly detected** on screen | **76.2 – 82.8 %** |
| Positions **extrapolated** | 17.2 – 23.8 % |
| Mean `predError`, all positions | 1.52 – 1.86 ft |
| Mean `predError`, detected positions | 0.84 – 0.91 ft |
| Mean `predError`, extrapolated positions | **4.36 – 5.15 ft** |
| 95th percentile `predError` | 4.88 – 6.28 ft |

**What this rules out.** Anything needing sub-foot precision: exact foot
placement, screen-contact point, whether a defender's hand altered a shot, true
release height. Ball `z` is delivered but ball calibration is explicitly less
accurate than player calibration, and can go slightly negative near the floor.

**What it supports comfortably.** Spacing measured in tens of feet, defender
distance in single feet, help arrival in seconds, and every zone-level
aggregation in this project. The independent validation is that our
nearest-defender distance reproduces SkillCorner's own `closestDefDist` to a
median 0.01 ft.

**How the pipeline handles the gaps.** Every spatial metric returns `NaN` rather
than a guess when fewer than three players are tracked. Derivatives are computed
inside continuous frame segments only and never across a dropout. Extrapolated
positions are flagged in the Tracking Lab so you can see exactly which players
were guessed at any frame.

Player `z` is always 0 by construction — the data cannot see a jump.

## 3. Dead time, frame gaps, and one property the source docs do not mention

**Roughly half of all video frames are dead time** (dead ball, warm-ups, breaks)
with empty player arrays and an empty ball object. Of 1,472,932 video frames
across the ten games, 829,704 carry player positions. The clock fields stay
populated on dead frames, so the timeline is continuous even where the tracking
is not.

**The video frame index is *not* continuous across period boundaries.** Measured
here and absent from the source documentation: every game has 3–4 frame-index
jumps, all of them exactly at a period change, ranging from **2 to 2,075 frames**
(game 114243 jumps 2,075 frames between Q3 and Q4). Within a period the index is
perfectly continuous. Any pipeline that assumes one unbroken timeline will
mis-align everything after the first quarter. The data-quality suite checks for
this explicitly.

**Frames inside a live possession:** 97.0 – 99.3 %. The remainder is inbound
setup and post-whistle play.

## 4. The season aggregates are offence only

This is a hard boundary, not a soft one. `acb_shotsaggregates`,
`acb_drivesaggregates` and `acb_picksaggregates` contain a player's own
shooting, driving and ball-handling production. There is **no defensive data at
season scale in this release.**

Therefore:

* Every defensive number in this platform comes from the ten tracked games:
  8–25 closeouts and 15–35 minutes of tracked assignments per defender.
* **No defensive rating is published.** Points allowed on a matchup conflates
  team scheme with individual defence, and at 20–60 chances per defender a
  single score would be noise wearing a decimal point. The Defensive
  Intelligence page reports volume, geometry and outcomes separately and refuses
  to collapse them.
* Player similarity and role clustering describe **offensive style only**.

Other aggregate caveats, from the source documentation and verified here:

* Coverage is 293 of 327 games (mostly the playoffs missing), so teams range
  from 31 to 34 games.
* `attempts` follows FIBA box-score convention and **excludes misses while
  fouled** — 41,684 shot events versus 38,029 `attempts` league-wide. Raw event
  counts will always run higher.
* `total_points` includes and-one free throws, so it is not
  `2 × fg2 + 3 × fg3`.
* The picks aggregate counts **only `direct = true` picks** — about 52 % of all
  pick events. Counting raw rows gives roughly double.
* `games_played` counts on-court presence, not games with a tracked event, so a
  row can have games played and zero attempts.
* Rows are per player-**team**-season; traded players also get a `team_name =
  'total'` row. Summing the wrong subset double-counts them. The app has an
  explicit toggle for this.

## 5. Player identity

Nine ACB players appear under two `player_id` values. All eight affected pairs
that reach the aggregates are **on the same team**, so a naive per-player
aggregation double-counts them — for example Jose Ignacio Vildoza (31 games, 183
attempts) and "Pepe Vildoza" (1 game, 1 attempt) are one person at Basquet
Girona.

This project handles it in two places:

1. Every event table gains a `<col>_id_canonical` companion column resolved
   through `player_id_aliases.csv`. **Aggregate on the canonical column.**
2. The season aggregates are additionally collapsed to `*_merged.parquet`:
   counts are summed; rates are **recomputed exactly** from the summed counts
   wherever the numerator and denominator both exist in the file.

The residue is honest: some rate columns in the picks and shots aggregates have
no reconstructable numerator/denominator pair in the delivered schema (101 of
them in shots, 278 in picks, 7 in drives). For the eight affected players those
rates are a **games-played-weighted mean**, which is an approximation. Every
other player's rates are untouched. The exact list is available from
`src.data.aliases.APPROXIMATED_RATES`.

Alias-merge totals are verified against the raw file in
`tests/test_schema.py::test_alias_merge_preserves_totals`.

## 6. Event classification is a model output

`picks`, `drives`, `handoffs`, `off_ball_screens`, `isolations`, `posts` and
`closeouts` are detections from SkillCorner's Game Intelligence models, not
human scoring. So are the coverage labels (`over`, `under`, `switch`, `ice`,
`blitz`, `soft`, `show`, `trail`, `whip`, `jam`, `top lock`) and the contest
levels. They have an error rate that is not published, and this project cannot
estimate it.

Practically: a "switch" that the model called and a coach would not is
indistinguishable here. Coverage-level conclusions should be read as "what the
detector saw", especially for the rare labels — `ice` appears 14 times and
`blitz` 4 times in 1,520 picks, which is why the app shows their counts and
declines to interpret them.

Measured marking noise in the ten games (all confirmed, none of it affecting
scores):

| Issue | Count |
|---|---|
| Passes where `passerId` equals the receiver id | 91 |
| Shots flagged `assisted` with no matching `assistOpp` | 38 |
| Missed field goals with no corresponding rebound row | 29 |
| Chances with zero elapsed game clock | 21 |
| Chances whose end frame precedes the start frame | 3 |
| **Orphaned `touchId` references** (1 drive, 5 passes, 1 dribble) | **7 of 24,487 (0.029 %)** |

The touch orphans are ours to report — they are not in the source
documentation. All touch-context joins are left joins, so those rows keep NULL
touch context rather than disappearing.

`passes.toReceiverId` holds the **intercepting defender**, not the intended
receiver, despite the name. It is used accordingly.

## 7. Metrics this project invents, and what they are worth

Each of these is labelled in the UI as derived here, never as a SkillCorner
metric.

| Metric | Status | Main weakness |
|---|---|---|
| **Expected FG (xFG)** | Validated: 5-fold grouped CV, AUC 0.689, beats base rate on log loss and Brier, well calibrated | SkillCorner's own `shotQuality` is more accurate (AUC 0.711). Ours exists for interpretability. Fitted on 1,322 attempts from one league |
| **Movement-intensity bands** | Descriptive | Thresholds (2/5/10/15/20 ft/s) are ours, chosen from the empirical distribution. Not SkillCorner physical-performance categories |
| **Roll / pop / stay** | Descriptive, 95 % coverage | The 4 ft threshold over 1.5 s is a judgement call. No ground truth exists to validate against |
| **Court control** | Analytical model, no external validation | Assumes straight-line travel, no turning cost, no acceleration limit, one speed for every player. A relative accessibility surface, not a probability |
| **Spatial advantage index** | Analytical model, **no validation at all** | Built on court control plus an analytic court-value curve. A descriptive lens. Do not quote it as a prediction |
| **Spacing metrics** (hull, nearest-neighbour, entropy, occupancy) | Straightforward geometry, reliable | Sensitive to tracking dropouts: five *tracked* players is not always five players |
| **Help-defender distance** | Reliable geometry | Operationalised as the second-nearest defender, which on a switch or stunt may not be the rotating player |
| **Player similarity / clustering** | Method sound, result honest | Offence only. Silhouette never exceeds 0.27 — the space is a continuum, so cluster membership is weak information |

## 8. What is deliberately absent

* **Any single-number player rating**, offensive or defensive. The data does not
  support one and combining unrelated statistics into a composite would hide
  exactly the uncertainty this document is about.
* **Lineup-level ratings.** `chances` carries the on-court five, but with ~100
  chances per team the per-lineup samples are single digits.
* **Season-scale defence.** Does not exist in this release.
* **Shot-contest quality independent of the shooter.** Not separable at this
  sample size.
* **Rebounding and passing models.** Assists and rebounds are tracking-derived
  judgement calls that the source documentation says differ from official
  scorers more often than shots do.
* **Any projection or forecast.** Ten games is a description, not a prior.

## 9. Reproducibility caveats

* The tracking files are Git LFS objects. Clone with `git lfs pull`, or fetch
  them from `media.githubusercontent.com` (see the README).
* `KMeans`, `PCA` and the CV splits are seeded, so clustering and model numbers
  reproduce exactly. Bootstrap CIs use a fixed seed per call.
* Package versions are pinned in `requirements.txt`. `scikit-learn` minor
  releases can shift logistic-regression coefficients in the fourth decimal.
* Total build time from raw files to a running app: about 5 minutes on 2 vCPU.
